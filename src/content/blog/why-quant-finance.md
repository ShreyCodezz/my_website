I'm starting a BS in Economic Sciences this month, and the honest answer to
"why quant finance" is that it's the rare field where math, code, and markets
all matter at once — and where the feedback loop on whether your thinking was
actually correct is unusually fast and unforgiving.

## what i'm doing about it now

Before classes even start, the plan has three legs:

- **Math foundations** — probability/statistics, linear algebra, and
  constrained optimization, since these show up everywhere from portfolio
  theory to ML.
- **Competitive programming** — Codeforces, starting from the basics in C++,
  because the algorithmic thinking transfers directly to trading and quant
  research interviews.
- **Applied projects** — small backtesting and simulation projects to make
  the math concrete instead of abstract.

## the programs i'm aiming at

Longer term, the target is a top master's — CMU MSCF, Baruch MFE, Oxford MCF,
or LSE — or a direct route into the industry via strong competitive
programming and project credentials. I'll write more about how that
decision shakes out as I get further into year one.

## why not just go straight into finance from undergrad?

I could try to skip the master's and grind straight for internships, but
IISER doesn't have the recruiting pipeline that a target school does — no
campus interviews from prop shops, no alumni network at the funds I'd want
to work at. A strong master's is the highest-leverage way to buy that back.
It's not the only way in, but it's the one where I'm not depending on
someone giving me a chance nobody else got.

That said, I'm not treating the master's as a foregone conclusion. If the
next three years of competitive programming and projects end up strong
enough on their own — the kind of portfolio a quant desk would actually
look twice at — I'll reassess. The point of doing the algorithmic and math
work now isn't to pad an application; it's to find out, as early as
possible, whether I actually enjoy and am good at this before I've sunk
years into a plan built on a career I've only read about.

## what "doing something about it" actually looks like week to week

Right now that's unglamorous: problem sets, Codeforces ratings that go up
slower than I'd like, and small scripts that break more often than they run.
None of it feels like quant finance yet. But I'd rather find out now, at
zero cost, whether I like the actual work — not the idea of it — than find
out in year three of a master's I paid for.

I'll check back in on this once I'm a few months into year one and have
something more than intentions to show for it.
