title: "school was just a phase"
description: "what i think about school"
date: 2026-04-18
tags: ["life"]
--- 

I just finished school, and honestly, I have no idea what I’m supposed to do next. For the past few years, everything was kind of decided for me. Wake up, go to school, study, give exams, repeat. There was always a structure, even if I didn’t like it. And now suddenly, it’s just… over. And everyone expects you to figure out your life like it’s obvious.

The strange part is, school never really teaches you how to grow in real life. It teaches you how to move through a system. You memorize things, write them in exams, get marks, and move on. But no one tells you how to actually build something, how to make decisions when things aren’t clear, or how to deal with uncertainty. And that’s literally what real life is.

Sometimes it feels like we all just go through this same path without really questioning it. Like it’s something we’re supposed to do, so we just do it. Subjects, exams, results. But when you step out of it, you realize how little of it actually connects to what comes next.

I’m not saying school is completely useless. It does give you some basics, and maybe some discipline. But it doesn’t feel like enough to rely on for something as big as your future. And that’s where the confusion starts.

Because now there’s no fixed path. No one telling you what chapter to study or what question will come. It’s just choices. And I don’t think I’m the only one who feels lost at this point.

But at the same time, there’s something a bit exciting about it too. For the first time, it feels like what I do actually depends on me. Not on a system, not on marks, but on the choices I make and the things I decide to learn.

I still don’t have everything figured out. I don’t even have a clear direction yet. But I do know that I don’t want to just follow another system blindly. This time, I want to understand what I’m doing and why I’m doing it.

Maybe that’s how it’s supposed to be. You don’t get clarity first, you get it by trying things, messing up, and slowly figuring it out.

So yeah, I’m confused. But I think that’s part of it.

And maybe, for the first time, that confusion actually means I’m starting to think for myself.
